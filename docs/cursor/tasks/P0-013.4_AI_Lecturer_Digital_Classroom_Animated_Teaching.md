# P0-013.4 — AI Lecturer Digital Classroom & Animated Teaching Experience

## 1. Objective

Transform the P0-013 AI Lecturer from a text/chat-oriented interaction into a **real digital classroom teaching experience**.

The AI Lecturer must behave like a professor teaching a student **step by step**, using a digital teaching board and appropriate animated visualizations.

The primary experience must NOT be:

> AI generates a large text response → text is displayed → text is read aloud using text-to-speech.

The primary experience must be:

> **AI Lecturer plans the lesson → introduces the concept → explains one step at a time → writes/draws/reveals content on the digital board → animates diagrams/processes → uses 2D or 3D visualizations where educationally useful → pauses for interaction/checks understanding → continues teaching.**

Text, voice, and chat are supporting mechanisms. They must not become the main teaching interface.

---

# 2. Core Product Vision

SYS should feel like a **real AI professor teaching on a smart digital classroom board**.

A student opening a lecture should see:

- AI Lecturer teaching context
- digital board
- progressively appearing teaching content
- animated writing/drawing
- diagrams and illustrations
- mathematical/formula rendering where required
- animated processes
- 3D visualizations where spatial understanding is important
- highlights, arrows, callouts and annotations
- examples being constructed step by step
- lecturer narration synchronized with the visual explanation
- controlled transitions between teaching steps
- opportunities for the student to ask questions or check understanding

The interface should feel like a **lecture**, not a chatbot.

---

# 3. Teaching Experience Model

The fundamental teaching loop is:

```text
Lesson/Topic
    ↓
AI Lecturer Teaching Plan
    ↓
Concept Breakdown
    ↓
Visual Representation Selection
    ↓
Teaching Step
    ↓
Board/Animation/3D Visualization
    ↓
Narration / Explanation
    ↓
Student Understanding Check
    ↓
Next Teaching Step
    ↓
Example / Application
    ↓
Summary
```

The lecturer should not present the entire explanation at once.

A complex topic must be decomposed into **small teachable steps**.

For example:

```text
Topic: Newton's Second Law

Step 1:
Introduce force, mass and acceleration.

Step 2:
Display:
F = ma

Step 3:
Animate a block.

Step 4:
Apply force to the block.

Step 5:
Show acceleration increasing.

Step 6:
Change mass while maintaining force.

Step 7:
Visually demonstrate the resulting change.

Step 8:
Work through a numerical example.

Step 9:
Ask the student a short understanding question.

Step 10:
Continue based on the student's response.
```

The exact teaching content is domain-dependent, but the **step-by-step teaching philosophy is mandatory**.

# 5. 2D vs 3D Visual Intelligence

The AI Lecturer must determine the most appropriate representation for each concept.

### 2D should be used when:

- a flat diagram is sufficient
- the relationship is conceptual rather than spatial
- a flowchart is more effective
- a simple graph explains the concept
- a formula/equation is the main teaching object
- 3D would add unnecessary complexity

### 3D should be used when it provides meaningful educational value.

Examples include:

### Physics

- 3D vectors
- projectile motion
- force systems
- rotational motion
- mechanics
- optics
- wave propagation
- spatial coordinate systems

### Chemistry

- molecular structures
- atomic arrangements
- chemical bonding
- molecular geometry
- orbital/spatial relationships
- reaction structures where spatial representation improves understanding

### Biology

- cell structures
- organs
- anatomical systems
- DNA
- biological structures
- physiological processes
- spatial relationships between structures

### Mathematics

- 3D geometry
- surfaces
- solids
- coordinate systems
- vectors
- spatial transformations

### Engineering

- mechanical components
- machines
- mechanisms
- sectional views
- assemblies
- structural relationships

### Geography / Earth Science

- earth structure
- terrain
- geological formations
- atmospheric structures
- spatial geographic relationships

### Computer Science

Use 2D or 3D/animated representations where they genuinely improve understanding, such as:

- memory visualization
- process/thread relationships
- network topology
- system architecture
- data-structure operations
- spatial/interactive conceptual models

### Mandatory design principle

Do NOT use 3D merely for visual decoration.

Use:

> **the simplest visual representation that gives the student the clearest understanding.**

When spatial or structural understanding is difficult with 2D, the lecturer should prefer an interactive/animated 3D representation.

# 6. Visual Representation Selection

Introduce a conceptual **Visual Intelligence / Representation Selection layer**.

The AI Lecturer should determine:

```text
Concept
   ↓
Educational Representation Decision
   ↓
TEXT / FORMULA / 2D DIAGRAM / CHART / ANIMATION / 3D MODEL
   ↓
Teaching Sequence
```

The LLM must not directly generate arbitrary frontend rendering code.

Instead, the AI service should produce a **provider-neutral structured teaching/visual specification**.

Example conceptual structure:

```text
TeachingStep

purpose:
    explain_molecular_geometry

visual_type:
    3D_MODEL

visual:
    model_type:
        molecule

    objects:
        atoms
        bonds

animation:
        rotate
        highlight
        zoom
        label

narration:
    explain the spatial relationship

duration:
    controlled by teaching sequence
```

The exact schema should follow repository conventions and be designed for extensibility.

# 7. Animation Architecture

Animations should be **instructional**, not decorative.

The system should support actions such as:

- reveal
- write
- draw
- highlight
- emphasize
- move
- rotate
- zoom
- pan
- transform
- connect
- separate
- compare
- replace
- fade
- annotate
- erase
- replay

Animations should be synchronized with the corresponding teaching step.

For example:

```text
Narration:
"Now observe what happens when the force increases."

Board:
    ↓
Force arrow becomes larger
    ↓
Object accelerates
    ↓
Acceleration indicator updates
    ↓
Lecturer explains the relationship
```

Avoid unnecessary animation effects that distract from learning.

# 8. Lecturer Narration

Voice narration may be used, but it must **not simply read a large generated text block**.

The lecturer's narration should correspond to the current teaching step.

Bad:

```text
Large paragraph generated by AI
        ↓
Text-to-speech reads paragraph
        ↓
Static screen
```

Required:

```text
Teaching Step
    ↓
Visual action
    ↓
Short contextual narration
    ↓
Visual emphasis
    ↓
Next teaching step
```

Narration should therefore be **synchronized with board actions**.

The system should be architected so that voice can be added/changed independently from visual rendering.

If the existing project does not yet have a production voice provider, use an abstraction/mock rather than hard-coding a provider.

# 9. Student Interaction

Student interaction must remain secondary to the teaching experience.

Provide a clear but non-dominant interaction mechanism such as:

- Ask Lecturer
- I don't understand
- Explain again
- Show another example
- Slow down
- Show visually
- Go back
- Continue
- Check my understanding

When the student asks a question, the lecturer should preferably respond through the **digital board teaching mechanism**.

For example:

```text
Student:
"I don't understand why acceleration increases."

AI Lecturer:
    ↓
Creates a focused teaching step
    ↓
Shows force vector
    ↓
Shows mass
    ↓
Animates acceleration
    ↓
Highlights relationship
    ↓
Provides short narration
```

Do not turn the response into a full-screen chat transcript.

```text

 ├── Introduction
 ├── Concept
 │    ├── Teaching Step
 │    ├── Visual
 │    └── Narration
 ├── Explanation
 │    ├── Teaching Step
 │    ├── Animation
 │    └── Narration
```

# 14. Backend / AI Service Boundary

The route/controller layer must not contain provider-specific LLM logic.

Use the existing AI/LLM abstraction.

The AI Lecturer service should be responsible for generating or selecting the structured teaching plan.

The frontend should be responsible for rendering that structured plan.

The architecture should conceptually be:

```text
Frontend
   ↓
Lecture Session API
   ↓
AI Lecturer Service
   ↓
Teaching Plan / Visual Specification
   ↓
Digital Board Renderer
   ↓
2D / Animation / 3D Renderer
```

Do not allow the API route to become an LLM-specific implementation.

# 15. 3D Rendering Architecture

Where 3D is required, use a maintainable frontend visualization architecture.

The implementation must:

- isolate 3D rendering from business logic
- allow reusable 3D teaching components
- support camera control
- support object highlighting
- support animation
- support labels/annotations
- support basic interaction where educationally useful
- avoid embedding model-specific logic in API routes
- allow future replacement/extension of the rendering technology

Do not introduce a large unrelated graphics framework without first checking the existing frontend stack and dependencies.

Prefer an implementation that fits naturally into the existing Next.js architecture.

---

# 16. Performance

The digital classroom must remain usable on typical student hardware.

Consider:

- lazy loading of heavy 3D assets
- progressive loading
- fallback rendering
- avoiding unnecessary GPU-heavy effects
- efficient animation
- reusable visual components
- reasonable asset sizes

If a 3D representation cannot be rendered effectively on a device, provide a meaningful 2D/animated fallback where possible.

---

# 17. Accessibility / Learning Support

The system should support students who may not be able to rely entirely on visual/voice presentation.

Where appropriate:

- narration transcript should be available
- visual explanations should have accessible descriptions
- important formulas/content should remain readable
- controls should be keyboard accessible
- animations should be pausable
- students should be able to replay explanations

Accessibility support must not convert the main interface into a text-chat experience.

# 20. Example Experience

For a topic such as **Human Heart — Blood Circulation**:

The lecturer should NOT display:

> "The human heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood..."

followed by a text-to-speech reading of the paragraph.

Instead:

### Step 1
Digital board displays a simplified heart.

Lecturer introduces the heart.

### Step 2
Heart chambers appear progressively.

### Step 3
A 3D heart model is introduced if spatial understanding benefits from it.

### Step 4
The model rotates slowly.

### Step 5
The lecturer highlights the right atrium.

### Step 6
Blood flow is animated.

### Step 7
The lecturer highlights the next chamber.

### Step 8
Flow continues through the heart.

### Step 9
The lungs are introduced.

### Step 10
The circulation path is animated.

### Step 11
Student is asked a short understanding question.

### Step 12
Based on the response, the lecturer continues, repeats, or gives another visual explanation.

This is the target experience.
